Key findings

Six findings from the 2026 Best Workplaces™.

Each finding below stands on its own, with its source. Link to or quote any of them directly.

  1. 25.8%

    Pride is the strongest culture signal for retention in Australia. Of everything the five Trust Index™ themes explain about whether employees intend to stay, pride accounts for 25.8%, the largest share.

    Independent retention study, 140,251 responses from 682 Australian organisations, 2025 to 2026.
  2. 96.2%

    96.2% of employees at Australia’s Best Workplaces™ 2026 are proud to tell others they work there, against 60.0% at typical Australian workplaces.

    2026 Trust Index™ survey, 128,405 Australian employees.
  3. 92.5%

    92.5% of employees at Australia’s Best Workplaces™ say management’s actions match its words, against 49.6% at typical Australian workplaces. The statement ranked in the top three of 60 for its link to a great culture at every company size and age group.

    2026 Trust Index™ survey.
  4. 93%

    Australia’s Best Workplaces™ 2026 average a Trust Index™ score of 93%, against 84.9% for Great Place To Work® Certified™ organisations and 57.8% for typical Australian organisations.

    2026 Trust Index™ survey, average positive response across 60 statements.
  5. 86.1%

    86.1% of employees aged 25 and under at Australia’s Best Workplaces™ want to work there for a long time, against 91.7% across all Best Workplaces™ employees.

    2026 Trust Index™ survey.3
  6. 59.9%

    The lowest-scoring statement at Australia’s Best Workplaces™ 2026 is “I feel I receive a fair share of the profits made by this organization”, at 59.9% positive.

    2026 Trust Index™ survey.
How culture is measured

Best Workplaces™ lead where it matters most.

Great Place To Work® measures culture across five themes.

Camaraderie, credibility, respect, fairness and pride.

An independent study tested all five against whether employees intend to stay long term, using two years of Australian Trust Index™ data. Of everything the five themes explain about retention, pride accounts for 25.8%, the largest share by far.2

What is the Trust Index™ survey?

The instrument behind every figure on this page.

  • Employees respond to 60 statements about their day-to-day experience at work.
  • Your people answer directly to us, and leaders see grouped results.
  • The Trust Index™ score is the average positive response across all 60.

Culture and Gen Z

Your youngest people can be your loudest advocates.

Employees aged 25 and under are the most likely to say they’re proud of where they work. They are also the most likely to be thinking about what comes next. Ask them what makes their workplace good and they talk about people: the team is the subject they name most in their written comments, ahead of pay, managers and perks put together.

What to do next

Put these results to work.

Insights matter when they change what you do. Here is where each leader can start.

HR leaders

Your top retention lever is pride.

Pride accounts for 25.8% of what culture explains about whether people stay. At the Best Workplaces™, 96.2% are proud to say where they work, against 60.0% at typical workplaces.

  1. Measure pride in your next survey. Start with “I’m proud to tell others I work here.”
  2. Look closely at who disagrees. 38% of people who strongly disagree say they are very unlikely to stay.
  3. Check that managers keep their word. 92.5% at Best Workplaces™ say they do, against 49.6% at typical workplaces.

CEOs and founders

Consistency is your fastest lever.

“Management’s actions match its words” ranks in the top three for its link to a great culture, at every company size and every age group. Pride, fairness and credibility all build on it.

  1. Ask your people about your top 20 leaders. Do their actions match the culture you describe?
  2. Make it the agenda at your next offsite. Align what leaders do with what they say.
  3. Track it between surveys. A pulse check shows whether it is moving.

FAQs

Questions about the report.

What exactly did you measure?

We sent the same 60-statement survey to 128,405 employees across Australia. It measures trust across five themes: camaraderie, credibility, fairness, pride and respect. The Trust Index score is the average positive response across all 60 statements.

What do you mean by pride?

Pride is measured by three statements: “I’m proud to tell others I work here”, “I feel pride in what I accomplish here” and “My work has special meaning”. Of what the five themes explain about retention, pride accounts for 25.8%.

Why should I trust Great Place To Work data?

The Trust Index methodology has measured workplace culture for 30 years in more than 150 countries. For the retention findings, an independent research partner ran the analysis on 140,251 Australian responses from 682 organisations.

Will Certification or a Best Workplaces place help us hire?

It gives candidates proof of your culture that your employees provided. Australian job seekers say they are 15 times more likely to apply to a Great Place To Work Certified company.5
